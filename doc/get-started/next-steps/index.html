Next steps

Learn on a real robot or in simulation, customize the sensor, and share and cite what you build.

You have a sensor streaming data. What comes next depends on your goal. Each section below points to the page or project that owns that part of the ecosystem.

#Learn with touch

Learn on a real robot

Mount the sensor, collect visuo-tactile demonstrations and train policies, with LeFlexiTac on a low-cost arm or Touch in the Wild with a handheld gripper.

→

Tactile simulation

Build the FlexiTac simulation in Isaac Sim, replay tactile trajectories and record demonstrations in the browser. Plus VT-Refine.

→

#Customize the sensor

FlexiTac-Gen

Generate the two FPCs of a FlexiTac sensor for your own gripper, fingertip or hand, in the browser. Pick a preset or fit to a surface of your STL part, choose the resolution, and download manufacturing files. The source and docs↗ are in the hardware repo.

→

Learn FlexiTac

An interactive course on how a resistive tactile array senses, how it's scanned, and what the numbers mean. Useful before you change the hardware.

→

#Share what you build

FlexiTac grows through the people who build with it. Whatever you made, a mount, a fix or a whole robot, it helps the next person.

  • Fix or extend these docs. Every page has an Edit page link. Tested a new sensor/board combination, or found a step that tripped you up? Send a pull request.
  • Add your paper or project to Awesome FlexiTac↗ by opening an issue or PR↗ (see its contributing guide↗).
  • Show your build on the Discord↗: photos, CAD for your mount, firmware settings and what didn't work are all welcome.
  • Report bugs in the repository that owns the code (see Still stuck?).
  • Star FlexiTac on GitHub↗ so others can find it.
